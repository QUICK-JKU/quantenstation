Paths into
quantum physics

There is no single path. There are three doors that all lead into the same room.

Physics

The question: What is actually going on? You work with real systems — atoms, photons, solids — and with how to control them in the lab. Lots of theory, lots of experiment.

Computer science

The question: What can you compute with it? You design algorithms, software and error correction. For this path you don't need to know how a qubit is built — but what it can do.

Mathematics

The question: Why does it work? The structure behind quantum mechanics is linear algebra. Those who really understand it see connections that stay hidden to others.

At JKU these directions combine well — for example through Technical Physics, Computer Science or Mathematics degrees with suitable specialisations. You'll find the current curricula at jku.at.

What you really need

Important You have to be able to cope with not understanding something for three weeks — and keep going anyway.
Helpful Enjoying maths and programming. But you'll learn both during your studies too.
Not necessary You don't need top marks in maths. You don't have to be a “natural talent”. You don't have to know at 17 what you want.
To be honest
The first year of study is pretty tough in all three directions: analysis, linear algebra, mechanics, programming. Quantum courses only come much later.

What you can do right now

Calculating with complex numbers

Complex numbers are the language of quantum mechanics, and school usually teaches you only a little about them.

Do some programming

Python is perfectly enough. There are freely available libraries you can use to simulate quantum circuits on your own computer.

Ask questions

Today, for example. From the outside, universities often seem closed, but they really aren't: there are taster days, open lectures and people who love to explain. Just ask us about whatever interests you.

Are there actually jobs in this field?
Yes — and considerably more than pure quantum research alone offers. People who study physics, computer science or mathematics often end up in data analysis, software development, simulation or measurement technology. The way of thinking is in greater demand than the specific expertise.
Do I have to go abroad?
You don't have to. But physics is an international field, and a semester or a PhD abroad is common. By the way, Austria has a very good reputation in quantum research.
Isn't it all just hype?
Partly, yes. Some promises are clearly exaggerated, and nobody knows for sure today which applications will catch on. But the physics behind it has been thoroughly confirmed for a hundred years — and the ability to control individual quantum systems precisely is new and real.
