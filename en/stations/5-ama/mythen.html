Six
quantum myths

Hardly any field of physics is misquoted as often. Six classics — and what's really behind them.

Myth 1
“A quantum computer tries all solutions at the same time.”
Actually
All possibilities are in play at the same time, but you may only measure once — and you get a random answer. It only becomes useful if you first cancel out the wrong possibilities through interference. That's exactly where most ideas for quantum algorithms fail.
Myth 2
“Consciousness makes the wave function collapse.”
Actually
No conscious observer is needed — a detector, a photographic plate or a single molecule of air is enough. What matters is the interaction with the surroundings. The idea was speculation by individual physicists and became famous through popular books. It is not supported by evidence, and today it plays no role in physics.
Myth 3
“Entanglement allows communication faster than light.”
Actually
Provably not. What you see on your side is pure chance — you can't choose the result, so you can't put a message into it. The agreement only becomes visible when both sides compare their results, and that only works over an ordinary channel.
Myth 4
“The uncertainty principle is down to our imprecise measuring devices.”
Actually
No — it also holds for perfect devices. Position and momentum aren't just hard to measure at the same time, they don't exist as sharp values at the same time. That's a statement about nature, not about our technology.
Myth 5
“Quantum physics shows that anything is possible.”
Actually
The opposite. Quantum mechanics is one of the most precise theories there is — some predictions agree to twelve decimal places. It doesn't allow everything; it prescribes very precisely what may happen and with what probability.
Myth 6
“Quantum physics is too hard to understand.”
Actually
The mathematics is demanding, but the central ideas are accessible: possibilities overlap, they can cancel each other out, and measuring changes the system. You've tried three of them yourself today — that was the hard part.
The best test
Real physics makes predictions with numbers that could turn out wrong. Where “quantum” only appears as a mood word — in healing, energy fields, consciousness — it's almost always marketing.
