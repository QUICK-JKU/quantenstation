What's behind it?

Three waves are superposed until the target curve is reached, and they only add up with the right phase. It is exactly these phases that the gates in a quantum computer adjust.

Waves don't add up like numbers, but with their phase. When crest meets crest, it gets louder or brighter — constructive interference. When crest meets trough, nothing is left — destructive interference. A quantum computer computes in exactly this way: it aligns the phases so that the wrong answers cancel out and the right one remains.
