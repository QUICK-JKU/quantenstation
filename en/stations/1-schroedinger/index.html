What is superposition?

A classical bit is 0 or 1. A quantum particle can be both at once until someone measures it. That is not a lack of knowledge about a value that was fixed long ago, but a state of its own. And that very state can be used for computing.

Quick questions

Is the cat really dead and alive?
No — and that was exactly Schrödinger's point. He came up with the example in 1935 as a criticism: if you naively apply the quantum rules to big things, you get nonsense. For single particles, however, superposition is measurably, precisely true.
Why does the superposition collapse when you look?
Because “looking” is never passive. Every measurement couples the particle to its surroundings — this process is called decoherence. The bigger an object, the faster it happens: for a real cat in an unimaginably short time.
What is it good for?
Superposition is the engine of quantum computers: a qubit can be 0 and 1 at the same time, and together with interference this is where the computing power comes from. What a qubit looks like is shown at Station 2.
